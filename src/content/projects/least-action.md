### Motivation

I originally became interested in the principle of least action through a broader interest in the mathematical foundations of physics. What particularly fascinated me was the idea that seemingly different physical phenomena could be described using a single underlying principle.

The project began as a research report for A-level Physics, but I became sufficiently interested in the topic to take the investigation considerably beyond the original scope. I wanted to understand not only the physical intuition behind the principle, but also the mathematical framework that makes it work.

Starting with problems such as the brachistochrone and Fermat's principle, I followed the development of action through the work of Maupertuis, Euler and Hamilton, eventually arriving at the calculus of variations and the Euler–Lagrange equation.

What began as an exploration of classical mechanics led me to some much broader questions. I was particularly interested in how the same framework could recover Newton's laws while also appearing in the foundations of quantum mechanics through Feynman's path integral formulation. I found the connections between these different areas especially compelling, and wanted to understand why the principle provides such a powerful way of describing physical systems.

### Additional skills

The report was written and typeset in LaTeX, which I learned specifically for this project to present the mathematical notation, equations and derivations clearly.
